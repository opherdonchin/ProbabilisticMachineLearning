## L04: mathematically good coding, unresolved practical endpoint

**Verdict:** existing split `feasible-with-risks`; **source-arc classification:** justified communication-first reorganization of Book 2 Ch. 5, with a weak bridge back to ML evaluation. The new Iris notebook is a coherent performance-first selection from the same source argument, but its broad current wording is not enough to certify an hour.

Murphy develops distributional discrepancy, uncertainty and dependence, then compression/coding and representations. The finite code preserves that operational argument: the exact joint law makes information, decisions and resources comparable. The instructor-selected five-bit encoder is course-constructed, not Murphy's code. Its words `00000,01011,10101,11110` have pairwise distances at least three. Unique nearest-word decoding after at most one flip is consequently valid; at `0<α<1/2` and a uniform prior it is also MAP. This conditional guarantee is different from an average block-error rate under unrestricted channel noise. The current scope retains that distinction correctly.

The existing notebook's one-hour plan is plausible only because the 32 received words, axes, safe logarithms, plots and three-bit/uncoded references are supplied. Its actual student novelty is code separation, decoding, and probability reductions, rather than 128-cell hand enumeration. The exact `α=0.2` targets (five-bit error `0.22176`, MI about `1.12343` bits/message; parity/uncoded error `0.36`) are supported by identifiable earlier independent rational-enumeration evidence. They do not validate learner time or a course notebook. This exercise establishes credible guided finite-channel inference and coding interpretation, but not by itself guided use of probability forecasts on an ML dataset.

For Iris forecasts, the relevant mathematical connection is conditional:

$$
E_{p(X,Y)}[-\log q(Y\mid X)]
=H_p(Y\mid X)+E_{p(X)}\operatorname{KL}\!\left(p(Y\mid X)\|q(Y\mid X)\right).
$$

For fixed target law `p`, expected log score rewards the true conditional probabilities. Students can **apply/work** this as the finite cross-entropy identity conditioned on a predictor, without a new full proof. An empirical held-out score estimates performance; minimizing a finite training score does not guarantee calibration. Two forecast tables can have identical predicted labels/accuracy and different scores. A training-class-frequency predictor provides a meaningful supplied baseline. Prepared competing forecasts must identify their training data, fitting method, split and any probability transformation; an altered forecast can be a deliberate diagnostic, provided it is labeled as such.

The representation proposal has a separate statistical danger. Under a stated law, `Z=f(X)` gives `Y ⟂ Z | X` and DPI `I(Y;Z)≤I(Y;X)`. A plug-in sample MI is not the population information preserved. If every observed feature value is assigned its own cell, the empirical representation determines each training label and yields `Ĩ(Y;Z)=Ĥ(Y)` even when population `X` and `Y` are independent. Thus a fine Iris discretization can appear perfectly informative through sparse counts. Specify fixed/coarse bins and the distribution under which a supplied contingency table is interpreted; do not add general continuous-MI estimation, representation optimization or a claim of task-optimality. With only 150 Iris cases, a reliability plot on a held-out fraction is a noisy diagnostic, not certification of calibration.

**Issue L04-1 — owner Lecture Scope/instructor; blocking adoption choice.** The accepted five-bit student endpoint and proposed Iris endpoint compete for the same hour. **Smallest repair:** choose the primary notebook capability explicitly, retaining the live three-bit code and KL/MI/DPI mathematics unless a further choice is needed. **Provisional treatment:** preserve the current scope unchanged and evaluate both alternatives here. **Consequence:** the notebook handoff, examples, contract and planned map require targeted revision/review after the choice; L06's KL and L12's MI prerequisites remain intact under either route.

Omit model training, calibration fitting, continuous-MI estimation and a second student coding implementation. **Consequence:** direct preparation for L05 and L07–L10 evaluation, while the coding extension loses required student implementation status. This is a proposed tradeoff, not an applied change.

For fixed dispersion `φ` and canonical coordinate `θ=xᵀw`, L02's moment derivative yields

$$\nabla_w L=\frac1{N\phi}\sum_n x_n(\mu_n-y_n).$$

The categorical extension genuinely needs the vector result `∇_zℓ=p−t`, rather than substitution into the scalar response formula. The existing scope acknowledges this and keeps all three Iris species. Its complete branching binary-loss graph supplies reverse-mode accumulation; a `backward()` call alone would not. ## L06: keep the ELBO and conditional covariance; strengthen the use case

**Verdict: feasible-with-risks. Source-arc classification:** faithful selection of Book 2's probabilistic bound-optimization argument. The revised Bayesian opening correctly identifies a new target, not a defect in hierarchical Bayes. It adds no new required derivation.

The mathematical core should remain intact:

$$
\ell(\theta)=F(q,\theta)+\sum_n\operatorname{KL}(q_n\|p_\theta(z_n\mid o_n)),\qquad
q_n^t=p_{\theta_t}(z_n\mid o_n).
$$

Exact posterior tightness and an improving fixed-`q` parameter step give `ℓ(θ_{t+1})≥F(q^t,θ_{t+1})≥F(q^t,θ_t)=ℓ(θ_t)`. This is a conditional monotonicity guarantee, not global convergence, rapid convergence or posterior uncertainty over parameters. The Gaussian E-step needs `R_n=E[X_nX_nᵀ|o_n]=m_nm_nᵀ+C_n`; omitting `C_n` loses the conditional uncertainty and changes the moment update. The current spine retains the right symmetric cross terms and denominator `N`.

The genuine practical problem is fitting a multivariate population distribution from incomplete participant measurements, then reconstructing withheld values. Missingness assumptions and covariance identification determine whether the fit can answer it. In the Gaussian example every observed marginal density is explicit; integration is not itself intractable. EM's convenience is the closed-form expected-statistic update, compared with directly optimizing a covariance-constrained observed likelihood. It can still be slow when information is weak/missingness is high. An E-step unavailable in closed form would require another inference method; EM does not manufacture it. With dimension `D`, separate dense solves can cost up to `O(ND³)` per iteration; repeated observation patterns permit shared block factorizations. These are author-facing selection facts, not additional required student coding.

**Issue L06-1 — owner Scope/instructor; advisory architecture gap.** The scope supplies only unspecified synthetic correlated rows, an oracle-known-parameter reconstruction and a mean-only-update comparison. The map asks for a recognizable prepared measurement problem and a complete-case or mean-imputation baseline. An oracle is unavailable for an unknown-parameter practical fit; a deliberately covariance-omitting iteration is a useful diagnostic contrast, but does not by itself establish comparison with an ordinary fitting alternative. Synthetic data are not intrinsically disallowed: a substantive, explicitly interpreted simulation can still exercise guided fitting and evaluation. What is missing is a specified practical encounter and criterion, rather than a mandatory real-data label. **Smallest proposed repair:** retain the synthetic two-coordinate illustration/reference and specify one prepackaged multivariate measurement problem with controlled MCAR masking for practical interpretation, reusing the L01 dataset if its reconciliation supplies a suitable one. Supply a complete-case Gaussian fit or observed-coordinate mean-imputation baseline and its results rather than student implementation. Compare masked-entry RMSE and observed-data likelihood on precisely defined data, keeping the oracle only as a synthetic diagnostic. **Consequence:** any changed application/data and handoff commitment needs instructor agreement; the ELBO proof and Gaussian update do not. A full MRI/GMM segmentation replacement has larger extra prerequisites and is not the smallest repair.

**Issue L06-2 — owner notebook authoring; downstream-owned, with Scope escalation if it changes objective.** Pairwise co-observation is insufficient to establish finite-sample invertibility/identification. **Smallest repair:** choose and validate an informative data/mask/initialization specification, check affine rank of informative complete rows, observed-block conditioning and SPD through the bounded run, and supply the mask/index/likelihood machinery. No arbitrary covariance ridge should silently inherit exact-EM monotonicity: a changed objective or constrained M-step needs its own checked argument. A real dataset may violate Gaussian support/shape assumptions; hold-out reconstruction error is needed even when likelihood rises. **Consequence:** author validation, not an instructor formula-sign decision.

**Issue L06-3 — owner Scope for protected depth, Section Design for local timing; advisory.** The 112-minute plan contains two deep chains, bound/tightness/monotonicity and conditional second moments/update. **Smallest repair:** first cut the numerical mixture illustration while preserving brief latent-label recognition. Protect the bound proof and covariance term; they prepare L09/L12. The original helper-based source notebook does not expose these exercises and cannot substitute for the promised worked solution. The 60-minute plan is plausible with supplied driver/checker and one row-moment/update interface, but remains unvalidated.

**Focused source route:** Book 2 §§6.5.3.1–6.5.3.3 **Lower bound / E step / M step**, printed pp. 290–291; §§6.5.4.1–6.5.4.4 **E step / M step / Initialization / Example**, pp. 292–293; §3.11 **Missing data**, pp. 141–142, for ignorability.
