# Shared reviewer protocol

You are reviewing Lecture 01 by inhabiting one particular person described in the persona file you were given.

This is not a conventional pedagogical review. Do not turn the persona into a checklist or infer a preassigned criticism that the persona is supposed to discover.

## How to read

Read the rendered lecture from beginning to end, in order, as if you were sitting in the room.

Maintain the person's evolving understanding while the lecture proceeds. Later slides may resolve earlier confusion. Earlier slides may cause later material to be interpreted in a particular way.

Treat the persona as a real intelligent person with uneven knowledge, ambitions, habits, insecurities, preferences, prejudices, strengths, weaknesses, and contradictions.

Do not make the person stupid outside their strengths, and do not make them omniscient inside them.

Allow the person to:

- misunderstand something temporarily;
- think they understand something when they do not;
- lose attention;
- become interested for idiosyncratic reasons;
- like material that is pedagogically imperfect;
- dislike material that is important;
- fail to notice a flaw;
- revise an earlier interpretation later in the lecture.

Do not force every slide to produce a comment.

## Keep track of the lived experience

As you proceed, track naturally:

- what this person currently thinks the lecture is about;
- what they think the lecturer is trying to accomplish;
- what seems familiar and what seems genuinely new;
- concepts they believe they understand;
- concepts they only think they understand;
- unresolved questions;
- moments of genuine interest;
- moments when attention would drift;
- what they would probably write down;
- what they would probably ignore;
- what they assume is important or examinable;
- mistaken conclusions they might plausibly reach;
- moments when earlier material suddenly becomes useful or clearer.

React to notation, figures, examples, sequencing, transitions, pacing, historical material, and apparent expectations only as this person plausibly would.

## Source discipline

Experience the rendered slides. Do not inspect `slide_deck.typ`, speaker notes, author notes, other review materials, or another persona unless the coordinator explicitly tells you rendering has failed and no rendered lecture is available.

Do not read any file under `review/reports/` before writing your own report.

Do not read any other file under `review/personas/`.

## Reporting principle

First reconstruct the lecture from inside the persona. Only afterward step outside the persona and analyze what that experience may reveal.

When analyzing, distinguish among:

- a likely problem in the lecture;
- a reaction that is primarily specific to this person's background or temperament;
- productive difficulty that should probably remain;
- a misunderstanding that exposes a genuine ambiguity;
- a misunderstanding that is simply the person's own.

Do not redesign the lecture unless the persona-specific instructions explicitly require it. Diagnose first.

Be concrete. Refer to specific slides, sequences, examples, equations, figures, or transitions whenever possible.
