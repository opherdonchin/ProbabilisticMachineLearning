## 3. Produce a new rule-guided initial design

Use R01–R15 proactively to design a coherent mathematical development from beginning to end.

In particular, pay attention to:

- establishing the mathematical question before introducing machinery;
- introducing only the notation that contributes to understanding;
- making consequential mathematical transformations and their justifications visible;
- keeping related equations, matrix operations, and cancellations visually connected;
- using layout, alignment, and signaling to communicate mathematical relationships;
- introducing progressive reveals when they support the reasoning;
- balancing mathematical detail against visual and cognitive load;
- making the meaning of the resulting equations clear.

These are not merely criteria for reviewing the finished slides. They should actively influence how you construct the mathematical explanation.

The rules are guidelines, not inviolable constraints. When they conflict, use mathematical and pedagogical judgment and document meaningful exceptions.

## 5. Document how the rules influenced the design

Create a **selective rule-application ledger** during design.

For consequential design decisions, document:

- The slide or reveal where the decision appears.
- The relevant numbered rule or rules.
- The actual design decision.
- Why it should improve the mathematical explanation.
- Any meaningful alternative considered.
- Any deliberate exception to a rule.
- Whether the intended effect is actually visible in the rendered slide.

Keep this record useful for debugging. Avoid a ceremonial checklist claiming that every rule has been satisfied.

The goal is to make it possible for us to distinguish failures of rule selection, rule application, rendering, and the underlying mathematical exposition.
