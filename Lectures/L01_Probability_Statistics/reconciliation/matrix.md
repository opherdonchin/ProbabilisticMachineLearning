## Decision-relevant mathematics

**Direction accepted in principle:** The instructor's subsequent “Sounds good” endorses aligning the three main questions with summarization, marginalization and conditioning and relating them to inference, prediction and generation. For one fixed $p(z,y)$, ask for a point reconstruction $E[Z\mid y]$, the distribution of new measurements $p(y)=\int p(z,y)\,dz$, and the distribution of hidden quantities $p(z\mid y)$, with a short activity generating new cases. The point reconstruction is a summary of the conditional distribution, so the operations naturally combine. Inference, prediction and generation describe uses of the model, rather than a second interchangeable three-operation list. Concrete context, mathematical depths, sequence and workload still belong to the developing Scope and notebook handoff.

Source check for that distinction: Murphy Book 2, December 10, 2025 online edition, Chapter 1, printed pp. 1–2, places inference methods in Part II and models for prediction, generation and discovery in Parts III–V. It describes predictive $p(y\mid x)$, generative $p(x)$ or $p(x\mid c)$, and latent-variable $p(z,x)$ with inference $p(z\mid x)$. These categories overlap in operations; they do not justify pairing summarization, marginalization and conditioning one-to-one with inference, prediction and generation.

For known jointly Gaussian missing and observed blocks, with invertible observed covariance,

$$
m(x_o)=\mu_m+\Sigma_{mo}\Sigma_{oo}^{-1}(x_o-\mu_o),\qquad
V=\Sigma_{mm}-\Sigma_{mo}\Sigma_{oo}^{-1}\Sigma_{om}.
$$

Under squared reconstruction loss, $m(x_o)$ minimizes conditional expected loss. In this correctly specified known-model experiment, its expected total squared error is $\operatorname{tr}(V)$; unconditional-mean imputation has expected error $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{mm})$. Thus the covariance reduction has an operational interpretation. A finite batch comparison illustrates this population result; it does not guarantee every reconstruction improves or validate Gaussian modeling on another dataset. Parameter fitting, model mismatch and missingness assumptions are distinct further issues.

This modest evaluation remains a possible supporting exercise rather than the notebook's central endpoint after the instructor's clarification. It can accompany the existing mathematical route. A substantive dataset would offer stronger modeling practice, but must be assessed separately for preparation and one-hour feasibility.
