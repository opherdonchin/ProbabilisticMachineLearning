## Comparative authoring evidence

All three variants use the course's ordinary mathematical type scale. The proof has not been reduced to make it fit; the risk identity, unique equality case, conditional-mean action, minimum risk, and Gaussian specialization remain whole. The side-by-side A2 experiment isolates geometry while holding content and slide boundaries fixed. In its completed state, the support/result direction is visible across columns, with the proof supporting a distinct right-side result and decision. Its cost is a denser result column and some horizontal eye travel. The vertical A2 experiment keeps the proof and result in one reading direction, but its completed vertical stack makes the risk identity, optimizer, Gaussian specialization, and lower takeaways compete for a single tall scan. Both fit; the difference is hierarchy and reading path, not rescued capacity.

B3 tests a different boundary: it gives the decomposition and residual-uncertainty interpretation their own slide before moving to the action decision. The decision slide has room for the recalled identity, optimizer, Gaussian action/minimum, and two takeaways as one complete job. This makes that decision easier to inspect locally; it also adds a slide transition and reorients students to the decomposition. No live pacing or classroom evidence was collected, so the attention and transition costs remain an authoring inference.

Actual slides 48, 59, and 61 were used as scale and hierarchy precedents. Their labels and mathematical structure show that normal-scale formulas and distinct proof/result roles are established course practice. The original actual-64 handout is reasonably legible; actual-65 combines its recall, moment work, risk identity, choice, Gaussian connection, and takeaways on one slide, producing competing full-width layers. The candidates improve the relationship between support and result or give the decision a separate boundary while retaining the full argument. They do not imply that a checked derivation alone establishes broader practical competence.

For every candidate, actual slides 63 and 66 match their frozen original-loss inputs pixel for pixel above the footer (the top 94% of each 1263×711 image). Their titles, keypoints, source notes, and mathematical body are preserved; the footer page counters differ because these are standalone review sequences. Thus the surrounding slide content is retained, while standalone numbering follows each candidate sequence.

Design and Organization inspected current variants and recorded their own decisions in their owning artifacts; the coordinator retains the independent-review and promotion stages.

The mathematical claim remains conditional: for a fixed observation (y), the displayed conditional risk decomposes into residual conditional variance plus squared distance from (m=E[z\mid y]), so the conditional mean uniquely minimizes model-expected squared error. The Gaussian specialization identifies (m=\mu_{z\mid y}) and residual risk (\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{z\mid y}). This establishes optimality under the stated conditional squared-error criterion, not a guarantee of empirical performance on future data. The expected quantities are assumed to exist; no checking-only qualifier was restored to projection. No speaker notes or production changes were made. These are isolated, instructor-authorized prototypes, not a promoted production edit.
