## Student state and transition map

### 63 → 64: a summary exists; optimality does not yet exist

Actual 63 ends with observed y → conditional Gaussian p(z|y) → summary z-hat = E[z|y] = mu_(z|y), and the integral defining that expectation. Its conclusions distinguish the whole posterior from a quantity extracted from it. It establishes neither a loss criterion nor optimality, nor the local abbreviations m,V,r. Earlier Gaussian work supplies mean/covariance concepts. A student can recognize the posterior mean but still needs to learn why choosing it answers a particular decision problem.

Calibration64 carries forward precisely the Gaussian posterior and mean identity. That is legitimate recall. It introduces a ∈ R^d, finite conditional second moments, loss ell(a,z)=||z-a||² and risk R(a|y)=E[ell(a,z)|y]. The role of a as a freely selected reconstruction of z is much less explicit than in original64; the symbols alone do not establish the contrast between the already displayed summary and a choice justified by a criterion. The key point finally says an estimate is judged by posterior expected loss, but never relates this to 63's mean summary. Thus mathematical continuity is repaired by literal carry-forward, while intellectual continuity remains partial.

Original64 explicitly stated the reconstruction task and that a is fixed after observing y. It was text-heavy around the inspected object, but those are meaningful conditions/connections, not expendable decoration. Calibration64's “y observed” plus a ∈ R^d leaves deterministic action status implicit; development65's specification mentions it in constraints but production never states it. The conditional expectation factorization later requires it.

### Candidate64 reveals: criterion → centered algebra → delayed criterion

- **64.1:** posterior recall plus action/condition and loss-risk definitions. No visible key point; a reserved key-point rule is already visible. This opening supplies symbols but weakly answers why 63's summary needs this criterion.
- **64.2:** the full expansion about mu_(z|y) appears as one emphasized two-line equation. This is a genuine new algebraic move and its cross term is intact. It does not cancel any expectation or prove optimality.
- **64.3/final:** emphasis ends and the criterion key point appears. This conclusion is supported by the loss/risk definition in 64.1, not by the newly dominant centered expansion. Nothing interprets why the expansion is centered at the conditional mean or what its three terms prepare us to evaluate.

The late reveal is therefore a weak reasoning transition: the final click returns to an earlier definition rather than interpreting the new move. One complete algebra reveal is reasonable; adding clicks for every algebraic motion would not solve the missing relationship.

### Candidate64 → candidate65: the stated recall is not the actual carry-forward

Candidate64 never defines m, C or r: it expands directly in mu_(z|y). Candidate65 starts with m=E[z|y], C=Cov[z|y], r=z−m and a risk expansion written in those symbols. The owning organization specifies V, not C. Three different student-visible roles are conflated: genuine recall of the risk definition; new local abbreviations; and a new result obtained by applying conditional expectation to the pointwise expansion.

In particular,

R(a|y)=E[||r||²|y]+2E[rᵀ(m−a)|y]+||m−a||²

was not established on candidate64. It is the first new proof step of 65. It uses linearity and the fact that m and a are fixed at the observed y. Candidate65 puts it inside the subordinate “Context / Prior slide” recall card (design lines 26–34; production opening card). The design rationale's claim that the centered expectation is a new proof object outside recall conflicts with the actual content assignment. This is an exact provenance and reasoning defect, not a stylistic objection.

The final Gaussian mapping m=mu_(z|y), C=Sigma_(z|y) comes only at 65.4. It retrospectively explains a notation change that students already needed at 65.1. Delaying a specialization after a general proof is sound, but delaying the correspondence with the actual predecessor while claiming recall is not. The pair needs one deliberately introduced abbreviation system, with the general meaning maintained throughout.

### Candidate65 reveals: new averaging hidden → cancellation → trace → optimization → mapping/conclusions

- **65.1:** recall card contains new notation and averaged expansion; simultaneously the main region gives E[r|y]=E[z|y]−m=0 and the cross-term factorization/cancellation. Compared with original65, this derives rather than merely asserts the zero mean and brings cancellation out of recall. However it gives the averaged expansion no status as new reasoning and offers no opening state that distinguishes it from the cancellation it licenses.
- **65.2:** residual energy becomes sum_j E[r_j²|y]=sum_j C_jj=tr C. This is a real new proof step, now visibly outside recall. Covariance is applicable because r is centered. The sum-to-trace equality is intelligible to the course audience; a full covariance derivation is unnecessary.
- **65.3:** intact R(a|y)=tr C+||a−m||², set-valued argmin={m}, and minimum=tr C appear together. Mathematically the optimizer follows from nonnegativity and equality only at a=m. The final-state source does not state that link in words; the squared norm supports it, but all three equations arriving together treats decomposition and reading off the optimizer as one compact beat. This can be viable if the implication is made legible, rather than inserting a missing theorem.
- **65.4/final:** repeats the whole Gaussian law, adds m,C mapping and z-hat, and reveals both prose conclusions together. The general claim “Gaussianity is unnecessary” is mathematically justified by the abstract proof but the preceding slide was presented solely in Gaussian mean notation. This final state asks students to integrate the delayed notation mapping, connection back to 63, and both interpretations at once.

The final state is not merely clipped or absent mathematics. It is a long succession of small equations at almost equal apparent weight: bordered opening expansion, unbordered cancellation, trace computation, risk equation, optimization line, repeated Gaussian law and identities. The risk equation declared dominant in design is only slightly differentiated in the actual render. Two long wrapped key points add substantial final density. Cumulative persistence is functioning mechanically; it does not itself organize the proof into a readable argument. Candidate handouts match the final content and retain the same difficulty.

## Viable two-slide organization — proposals, not adopted changes

The existing division is intellectually defensible: slide64 establishes the criterion and pointwise centered expansion; slide65 averages that expansion, evaluates its terms, derives the risk decomposition and reads off the optimizer. These are distinct jobs and both are needed at the accepted derive/prove depth. The candidate renders do not demonstrate that this division is impossible; they demonstrate poor management of shared notation, new-versus-old work and final hierarchy.

**Smallest organization repair proposal:** retain order, two-slide extent and current titles, but make the teaching-move contract specify (i) the link from 63's posterior summary to selecting an action under squared loss; (ii) introduction of m,V,r once on64, with Gaussian correspondence made explicit there rather than retrospectively reconstructed on65; (iii) the unaveraged centered expansion as64's endpoint; (iv) conditional expectation as65's first new operation, never recall; (v) the intact decomposition as the load-bearing equation connecting cancellation/trace to uniqueness/minimum risk and back to the Gaussian mean. This follows the existing owning notation contract and preserves all mathematical commitments.

**Optional title proposal, Slide Organization owned:** “Squared-loss risk starts with a centered error” would better advertise64's actual dominant algebraic work, while the current title stresses the criterion. This is not necessary if the current title and its two linked beats are made explicit. Do not change the supplied fixed title silently.

**Slide Design proposal:** make the dependency/term relationships visible, retain only genuinely needed carry-forward, and integrate the Gaussian identification compactly. This is a semantic relationship request, not a finished layout specification. Restore explicit model-expected-loss language in interpretation and avoid presenting repeated Gaussian-law notation as a second competing endpoint. Every required equation and the finite-second-moment regime must survive.

No Section Design or Lecture Scope change is presently justified. The 2–4 minute estimate is unmeasured; the candidates' excess notation and explanatory burden create avoidable time pressure, not evidence that the proof depth must be reduced. Recheck time after a coherent pair is rendered.
