## Mathematical audit

**Generic identity and proof:** `ell=F+ΣKL(q||posterior)` has the correct direction/sign. Exact posterior `q^t` makes the bound equal the observed likelihood at `theta_t`. Keeping that distribution fixed makes its entropy constant in candidate parameters, so maximizing expected complete log likelihood `Q` improves the same bound. The three-link chain is correct; an exact M-step is more than necessary, and a demonstrated increase in this same `Q` also suffices. The scope correctly withholds that guarantee from arbitrary optimizer steps, changed regularizers and non-touching approximate E-steps. It also avoids deriving both Jensen and KL proofs at full depth.

The `F`/`Q` separation is particularly useful: B2 Eq. 6.147 uses `Q` for an entropy-including bound, whereas its later Gaussian Eq. 6.152 uses `Q` for expected complete log likelihood. L06 deliberately chooses one consistent convention. `Q` without entropy is not generally a touching lower bound, especially for continuous latent values. The finite-quantity/support assumption prevents informal point-mass differential-entropy manipulations. The scope also avoids carrying B2/B1's stronger local-maximum convergence wording into a general theorem.

**Expected statistics and update:** assembling `m_n` in original row coordinates and placing conditional covariance only in the missing–missing block gives `R_n=m_nm_n^T+C_n`. Observed–missing cross moments are present, and complete rows have zero conditional covariance. Fully missing rows provide no observed likelihood information and are excluded from the lab; their current prior moments would otherwise be legitimate E-step quantities, not additional data evidence.

The symmetric quadratic expansion in `Q` is correct. B2 Eq. 6.156's `-2 mu E[y]^T` shorthand is harmless inside the scalar trace when treated appropriately, but is not a symmetric matrix equality; the scope's two cross terms avoid that ambiguity. The mean stationary condition and covariance moment-matching application yield `mu_new=Σm/N` and `Sigma_new=ΣR/N−mu_new mu_new^T`, with ML denominator `N`. A full matrix derivative is unnecessary for the promised treatment. PSD does not imply SPD, and that limitation is explicitly retained.

**Independent numerical check:** standard-library scratch arithmetic, separate from any notebook, reproduced the following course-constructed example:

- For `(2,missing)` under old mean zero and covariance `[[1,1/2],[1/2,1]]`, missing mean is 1, variance is `3/4`, and `R=[[4,2],[2,7/4]]`.
- Combining it with `(-1,-1)` and `(0,1)` gives mean `(1/3,1/3)` and covariance `[[14/9,8/9],[8/9,41/36]]`, determinant `53/54`.
- Direct observed marginal densities give old/new summed log likelihood `−7.640343926904915` and `−7.137887259555043`, increase `0.5024566673498718`. The third row contributes a one-dimensional marginal, not a density evaluated at an imputed second coordinate.
- Omitting `C` lowers the second-coordinate variance in this update by `(3/4)/3=1/4`. This is an exact local comparison, not a guarantee about every iterative baseline's error.
- The equal-weight scalar mixture responsibility at `x=1/2` is `sigmoid(1)=0.7310585786300049`, as claimed.

These numbers are correctly labeled course-constructed. No source figure outcome or source notebook accuracy is adopted as a required result.

**Missingness:** under the stated MAR model and distinct variation-independent missingness parameters, the realized mask likelihood depends on observed values and its own parameters, not missing values or `theta`; it can factor outside the missing-value integration. The resulting missing-value posterior and likelihood estimation for `theta` justify the selected EM calculation. MAR alone is not a universal ignorability license; the scope includes the needed parameter qualification and uses parameter-independent MCAR in the lab. MNAR is expressly outside this promise. In Gaussian observed likelihood, computing marginals can be easy even when maximizing the joint incomplete-data objective is inconvenient; the scope states this accurately.
