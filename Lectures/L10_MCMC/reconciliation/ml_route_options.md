## A. Smooth posterior geometry → HMC (recommended)

**Learning problem and endpoint.** Infer uncertain coefficients or hierarchical effects from a moderate-sized dataset, then estimate a predictive probability and a scientifically interpretable posterior-region probability. Observations induce a smooth continuous posterior; a grid becomes expensive as parameter dimension `d` grows, while full-data gradients remain affordable. Students should leave able to explain and apply an HMC transition, relate its trajectory cost to posterior scales, and distinguish a well-targeted algorithm from an adequate finite estimate. The reusable endpoint is *using geometry to design and assess computation*, independent of which investigated dataset later supplies the encounter.

For `E(θ)=−log u(θ)`, introduce momentum `p~N(0,M)` with fixed positive-definite **momentum covariance** M:

$$\widetilde\pi(\theta,p)\propto\exp[-H(\theta,p)],\qquad H=E(\theta)+\tfrac12p^TM^{-1}p.$$

**Derive** that marginalizing p returns π and that Hamilton's equations `θ̇=M⁻¹p`, `ṗ=−∇E` conserve H. **Work and justify** the leapfrog map

$$p_{1/2}=p-\tfrac\epsilon2\nabla E(\theta),\quad
\theta'=\theta+\epsilon M^{-1}p_{1/2},\quad
p'=p_{1/2}-\tfrac\epsilon2\nabla E(\theta').$$

Each substep is an invertible shear with unit Jacobian determinant; reversing momentum reverses the trajectory. Composing L steps with a momentum flip gives the reversible volume-preserving proposal used with `min(1,exp[H_start−H_end])`. **Derive** this energy correction from the bounded MH bridge, not a second general MH theorem. Momentum refresh avoids confinement to one energy level, but is not by itself a proof of irreducibility or rapid mixing.

The decision-relevant geometry calculation is unusually economical. For Gaussian covariance C, `E=½(θ−m)ᵀC⁻¹(θ−m)`,

$$\ddot\theta=-M^{-1}C^{-1}(\theta-m).$$

**Work** the eigen-directions: with `M=I`, the narrowest scale limits a stable leapfrog step; choosing `M=C⁻¹` equalizes the frequencies. This establishes why whitening/preconditioning can save gradient evaluations. It does not establish that a single covariance fixes varying curvature. **Work** one bounded change-of-variables example, including its density Jacobian if used; **state** NUTS as trajectory-length adaptation, and explain that funnel geometry, separated modes and large gradient costs remain real limitations. Do not derive NUTS, Riemannian HMC, several integrators or a general Jacobian system.

**Validity, payoff and remaining error.** Differentiability, proper target, correct gradients/transformations and a correct fixed-kernel construction matter. Any adaptation must be confined to a separately specified warm-up protocol before the assessed fixed-kernel draws; supplied adaptive software needs its own validated algorithm, not this fixed-L proof. Each trajectory costs about L full-data gradient evaluations and an endpoint full-target evaluation for MH correction (often dominated by gradients); for a dense `N×d` GLM, leading gradient work is `O(LNd)` per proposal, versus `O(Nd)` for a random-walk density evaluation. An identity/diagonal metric costs `O(d)` storage; dense M costs `O(d²)` and requires `O(d³)` factorization/setup, plus `O(d²)` operations per step. Retaining S parameter draws costs `O(Sd)`; many later query averages reuse them, with query-dependent precision. HMC pays only if improved exploration compensates for those costs. A two-dimensional quadrature reference can remain cheaper. Finite initialization/exploration error, autocorrelation and uncertain diagnostics remain; Metropolis correction addresses numerical-integrator target bias, not all of these. A Gaussian proxy may already be adequate for the requested prediction and cheaper for repeated draws.

## B. Exact structure inside MCMC → blocked posterior inference

**Learning problem and endpoint.** Infer uncertain regime histories and transition probabilities from a noisy sequence. L08's fixed-parameter HMM answers a conditional state question exactly, but uncertainty about dynamics requires integrating over parameters. The endpoint is to **construct a joint sampler whose expensive latent block is sampled exactly using message passing**, and recognize when marginalization or conditional averaging is preferable to sampling every unknown. This is more than single-site Gibbs recognition and has no mechanics prerequisite.

For observations y, states z and parameters θ, **derive** conditional invariance in a short integral and explain why composing the two conditional kernels preserves `p(θ,z|y)` even when a systematic sweep is not reversible:

$$\theta^{s+1}\sim p(\theta\mid z^s,y),\qquad
z^{s+1}\sim p(z\mid\theta^{s+1},y).$$

**Derive/work** the new backward sampling step, using L08's normalized filter `f_t(i)=p(z_t=i|y_{1:t},θ)` and transition A:

$$z_T\sim f_T,\qquad
P(z_t=i\mid z_{t+1}=j,y,\theta)
=\frac{f_t(i)A_{ij}}{\sum_k f_t(k)A_{kj}}.$$

This samples coherent histories; independently drawing each smoothed marginal does not. **Apply** supplied conjugate parameter conditionals (for example a transition row has Dirichlet parameters prior plus observed path transition counts), rather than derive a new emission-prior family. **Work** conditional averaging for one query: `E[g(z,θ)|y]=E_{θ|y} E[g(z,θ)|θ,y]`. **Derive** the law-of-total-variance inequality for independent draws, while explicitly stating that it does not universally rank asymptotic variance per unit cost of different correlated chains.

**State/interpret** collapsed Gibbs as a distinct operation: integrate parameters before updating latent assignments. Murphy's conjugate mixture gives `p(z_i=k|z_-i,x) ∝ (N_{k,-i}+α/K) p(x_i|D_{-i,k})`. Removing parameters can introduce dependencies between the retained variables. Do not promise to implement both an HMM blocker and a collapsed mixture. Collapse is a contrast explaining the choice of sampled state, not another complete model lesson.

**Validity/payoff/error.** Exact conditional path draws do not make joint parameter/path draws independent. Dense filtering costs `O(TK²)` per sweep, storage `O(TK)`; after filtering, backward sampling costs `O(TK)`. Parameter counts can be accumulated in `O(T)` and stored with the `K²` transition parameters. This avoids enumerating `K^T` paths and may move past single-site path dependence, but strong parameter/path coupling and label symmetries can still defeat the finite budget. Collapse need not improve sweep complexity: Murphy states both naive and collapsed mixture samplers have the same `O(NKD)` cost under his assumed operations. General full-covariance Gaussian predictive costs require more accounting. Neither lower dimension nor acceptance one guarantees faster effective inference.

**Extent and practice.** Provisional live costs: bridge/task `12`, conditional-kernel construction `12`, FFBS derivation/application `25`, parameter/path coupling and blocked comparison `14`, collapse/conditional averaging `17`, finite-query/error comparison and summaries/handoff `14`, reserve `16` = `110`. ## C. Additive data likelihood → scalable Langevin, with explicit bias

**Learning problem and endpoint.** Bayesian prediction for a GLM with large N and moderate d, where repeated full-data gradient/density evaluations dominate. The endpoint is to **derive a correctly scaled noisy-gradient posterior update and assess the tradeoff between per-step data work and posterior-query error**. It is a distinct scale-driven argument, not “HMC with a cheaper gradient.” No neural network is required before L11.

With `E(θ)=−log p0(θ)−Σ_n log p(y_n|x_n,θ)`, **derive** the unbiased uniform-minibatch gradient

$$\widehat g(\theta)=-\nabla\log p_0(\theta)
-\frac NB\sum_{n\in\mathcal B}\nabla\log p(y_n\mid x_n,\theta).$$

**State** the invariant-density result for the continuous-time Langevin diffusion `dθ=−∇E dt+√2 dW` under suitable regularity/confinement; do not require Brownian calculus or a Fokker–Planck proof. **Work** Euler discretization `θ'=θ−h∇E(θ)+√(2h)ξ`, then substitute the stochastic gradient to get SGLD. **Derive** its simplest caution: for `E(θ)=aθ²/2`, full-gradient Euler is an AR(1) chain with stationary variance

$$\frac{2h}{1-(1-ha)^2}=\frac1{a-ha^2/2},\qquad 0<ha<2,$$

where the target variance is `1/a`. Thus exact gradients and unlimited fixed-step draws still leave discretization bias. Conditional minibatch-gradient variance adds another noise contribution; it is not automatically the calibrated `2hI` noise. **State** MALA's full-data MH correction as an alternative which restores the target at additional data cost; **work** one control-variate gradient expectation only if replacing other depth. No SG-HMC, adaptive diffusion or nonconvex convergence catalogue.

**Validity/payoff/error.** A step now uses B observations, `O(Bd)` dense GLM work instead of `O(Nd)`, with `O(d)` state and `O(Bd)` batch storage beyond dataset storage/access. It does not establish fewer total observations processed for a required accuracy. Fixed-step discretization bias, minibatch noise, nonstationary step schedules, exploration and finite-query variance must be separated. Decreasing steps have asymptotic results under additional conditions, not a free finite-time guarantee; plain post-burn-in ESS formulas cannot simply be transferred to a decreasing-step run. Full-data checks or references themselves cost data passes. Ordinary SGD's gradient noise is state-dependent and anisotropic; it is not a general posterior sampler. The route gains large-data judgment while losing HMC and structured latent inference.

**Extent and practice.** Hypothesis: bridge/task `12`, scaling/gradient estimator `18`, diffusion result and discretization `20`, Gaussian bias calculation `16`, MALA versus SGLD cost/noise comparison `15`, query/comparator assessment and summaries/handoff `13`, reserve `16` = `110`. Roughly 7–9 new result families and 6–8 selected source pages; timing risk high because SDE notation and the distinction between two sources of noise are new. Notebook: supply data pipeline, reference, full-gradient and MALA runs and diagnostics; students complete the scaled minibatch gradient plus injected-noise update and compare posterior queries as h/B/data-access budget change. No selected existing candidate demonstrates this large-N payoff. A meaningful scaling study and same-query VI comparison need new validation, with supplied runs to protect an hour. Reusing a 23-flight dataset alone would not establish practical large-data competence.

**Reading route:** B2 §12.5.6 **Langevin Monte Carlo (MALA)**, pp.521–522, then §12.7 introduction and §12.7.1 **Stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD)**, p.533; §12.7.3 **Reducing the variance of the gradient estimate**, p.534, only if chosen. §12.5.7 **Connection between SGD and Langevin sampling**, pp.522–523, is instructor checking material with the sum/mean error below; it should not supply a broad generalization claim.

## Source corrections and finite-computation safeguards

Primary text actually inspected: Murphy, *Probabilistic Machine Learning: Advanced Topics*, online **December 10, 2025**; `/tmp/pml-book2.txt` SHA256 `1d77150146caf0f7e44b88b20370292abe5b6bfc506ce92fe26509a7bdb131b6`. Printed page numbers above are from its page headers. No Book 1 passage is needed as new core material. The source manifest and [existing computational report](../notebook_geometry/report.md) preserve exact PDF/source and real-model records; their MH results are not new HMC/SGLD evidence.

- **HMC convention:** Eq.12.64 uses `Σ⁻¹` in K but calls Σ inverse mass; Eqs.12.75–76 instead use Σ in K while the algorithm refreshes `N(0,Σ)` and moves with `Σ⁻¹p`. Those formulas cannot all hold simultaneously. Use the explicit M convention in A. Then Gaussian frequency matching gives `M=C⁻¹`, not `M=C`. Eq.12.69's simultaneous Euler expression also uses the new momentum despite the preceding simultaneous-update description; Euler variants need not be taught.
- **Energy error/divergence:** §12.5.3's prose says nonconstant energy means divergence/rejection and then applies MH only when energy is constant. Numerical leapfrog normally has nonzero energy error; the MH probability uses that error. A divergence flags serious integration trouble, not every nonzero energy difference. Do not copy that prose or promise that accept/reject removes poor exploration. §12.5.4.2's “square root of the smallest marginal standard deviation” is not a reliable scale rule: for a quadratic target the leapfrog stability condition follows the largest eigenvalue of `M⁻¹C⁻¹` (for identity M, step scales with the smallest principal standard deviation).
- **Collapse:** Eq.12.56's final numerator prints `N_{k,-i}+α`, inconsistent with its `α/K` prior and preceding gamma ratio; Algorithm12.2 correctly uses `α/K`. The count `N_{k,-i}=N_k−1` applies when i is currently assigned to k; generally subtract `1[z_i=k]`. Marginalization can improve an IID estimator's variance; it does not universally improve a correlated algorithm's variance per second.
- **Large-data gradient:** Eq.12.122's expanded expression has the wrong prior sign relative to12.119; its `B/N` factor inside the outer `N/B` does scale the prior once. The correct gradient estimator is displayed in C. §12.5.7 defines a summed loss but treats an unscaled minibatch mean as unbiased for its gradient; fix sum/mean conventions before reasoning about noise. Algorithm12.5 line5 prints a sum of energies where a snapshot **gradient** is required. These are direct consistency/algebra findings; inspect the PDF before quoting a typeset error as a facsimile claim.
- **Prior preserved cautions:** Under the variance convention, Eq.12.112 `Var(x|ν)=exp(ν)` requires `x=z exp(ν/2)`, not Eq.12.115's `exp(ν)`. Eight Schools uses standard errors of estimated effects. L09's checked Gaussian-KL corrections and the existing negative-autocorrelation warning remain relevant. For every route, show the query-specific stationary covariance sum or ESS/MCSE interpretation at **state/work** depth; its full pair-count derivation is relinquished. Conditions for LLN/CLT, nonstationary starts, unexplored regions and constant traces remain explicit. Negative correlation can reduce variance; constant/stuck traces do not establish precision.

For stationary finite-variance query values with autocovariance `γ_g(k)`, retain the precise assessment object

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar g_S)=\frac1S\left[\gamma_g(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{S-1}(1-k/S)\gamma_g(k)\right].$$

State/work this result and distinguish it from initialization or missed-region bias. Long-run ESS/MCSE requires the relevant moment, dependence and CLT conditions; an empirical diagnostic does not prove them. A fixed-h SGLD chain may estimate expectations under a biased invariant law, while a decreasing-step run needs schedule-specific analysis. The notebook should report query discrepancies against a reference or stated tolerance without inventing a scientific decision threshold.
