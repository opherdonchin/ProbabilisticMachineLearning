## Selection criteria

An in-class notebook should satisfy **four independent requirements**:

1. **Mathematics:** a substantive problem that puts the current lecture's concepts to use;
2. **Reference:** an inspectable, solved notebook or written worked solution;
3. **Complementarity:** an application that the lecture does not already solve (sometimes a textbook subsection is intentionally reserved for the notebook);
4. **Durable skill:** a concrete implementation ability students can reuse in later weeks, not just exposure to package names.

Students arrive with NumPy, PyMC, Bambi, ArviZ, probabilistic programming, hierarchical regression, NUTS, diagnostics and basic DAGs from undergraduate coursework. They **do not** need another routine exercise in using PyMC to sample or inspect R-hat. Their newer PyMC / modular ArviZ APIs may still require guidance. **Introduce PyTorch in L5 (review proposal)** and reuse it in L11–L12. **Do not adopt JAX** or introduce a JAX-only library as a required student dependency. Use SciPy and scikit-learn for reference implementations and conventional modeling workflows. A new library has to earn its curricular place.

## Workflow for lecture-specific preparation

Before a notebook becomes a course commitment, (a) decide exactly where lecture mathematics ends and application begins, (b) verify the referenced worked solution, (c) identify the single result students should obtain, (d) run and time the adapted solution in the supported environment, (e) explicitly list which computation packages and idioms are *new* and which may be assumed.

**Scope notes**: The Ising denoising proposal for L3 is conditional on making core undirected factorization a substantial advance beyond the undergraduate DAG introduction. The formal methods of MCMC and VI remain later topics, even if a simple conditional update appears in L3. L6 MRI vs Gaussian missing-data EM, L8 Kalman vs HMM, and L12 mobility vs MNIST are intentionally still open.
