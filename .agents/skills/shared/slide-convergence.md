# Coupled slide development

Use this protocol under Lecture Development coordination when developing a
substantial slide sequence, or when Organization, Design and Authoring expose
interdependent choices. Scope and Section Design retain their intellectual
contracts and normal lightweight feasibility checks. The coupled work concerns
making those contracts teachable through realistic slides.

## Establish which commitments can move

The coordinator identifies each constraint's owner and provenance before
negotiation: an explicit instructor decision, an approved upstream commitment,
a provisional stage choice, or a coordinator task-envelope restriction. Do not
turn a provisional slide count, title, persistence pattern or guessed timing
into an instructor requirement. Preserve the approved endpoint, mathematical
depth and live/recorded boundary. Escalate evidence that those commitments need
to change; do not routinely reopen Scope or Section Design.

## Keep the relevant specialists in the same exchange

Usually keep Organization, Design and Authoring active together for the affected
section/cluster; use two when the third stage is genuinely unaffected. They
retain separate ownership:

- Organization offers teaching moves, boundaries, titles/order and count options.
- Design offers visible arguments, object relationships, wording and reveals.
- Authoring offers implementation alternatives, capacity evidence and prototypes.

Anyone may propose a change; its owner decides how to revise the provisional
artifact. Direct peer exchanges are useful, with material offers, disagreements
and decisions returned to the coordinator. Agents do not concurrently edit the
same file or quietly adopt another stage's pedagogical decisions.

An offer states what it preserves, what it asks to change, why that could solve
the problem, what it costs, and which evidence would support or reject it. The
recipient returns an acceptance with conditions, a counteroffer, or a reasoned
rejection with a useful alternative. A verdict such as "too dense" alone is not
a negotiation. Distinguish a failed implementation from a claim that every
realization of a teaching move is impossible.

The coordinator circulates the actual alternatives to all affected owners,
resolves authority/provenance confusion, and asks for the response that will
reduce the current uncertainty. Do not merely relay a failure upstream and
reissue the same rigid brief. Keep a compact record of current alternatives,
agreements, evidence needed and the next experiment/owner; Git preserves old
attempts rather than accumulating competing current specifications.

## Use bounded prototypes before freezing coupled choices

A read-only `feasibility-review` remains a preview and creates no downstream
artifact. If geometry, mathematical grouping or reveal behavior requires a
render to judge, explicitly dispatch a bounded exploratory `build` or `revision`
with provisional inputs. Specify the uncertain question, exact content/version,
what may vary, what must remain, owner, isolated output location and evidence to
return. A final-state or difficult-reveal prototype may suffice before rendering
the whole cluster. Compare genuinely different alternatives where useful.

Attach each result to the realized content version, object-to-region/reveal
relationships and actual complete states inspected. A filename or candidate
label does not establish that its proposed arrangement was implemented. The
affected owners must confirm that realization before using fit evidence to
change the teaching sequence. A fitted state with missing mathematics is not
capacity evidence for the complete explanation.

This authoring experiment can precede formal design approval. It is neither a
production commission nor permission to publish or replace approved lecture
sources. Its results inform Organization and Design revisions; it cannot grant
approval or establish classroom effectiveness. Label guided negotiation and
builder prototypes accurately, rather than calling them independent validation.

## Converge on a coherent set of artifacts

Continue while offers or experiments can resolve a material uncertainty. The
specialists should converge on the same version: Organization's teaching moves
can be expressed by Design's visible argument; Authoring can realize that
argument with readable mathematics, coherent actual predecessor/reveal states,
and course-style hierarchy. Necessary proof warrants, key-point roles,
retrospective section endings and all accepted mathematical commitments survive.
A successful fit check alone does not establish this agreement.

Before accepting convergence, inspect actual render evidence and distinguish
remaining timing estimates and classroom uncertainties from checked output.
If exchanges repeat without new evidence, identify the unresolved tradeoff and
bring the concrete alternatives to the instructor; do not claim convergence or
keep retrying cosmetic variants. Instructor steering can stop or change this
loop immediately.

Submit the coherent provisional Organization/Design set through the existing
approval gates, then complete faithful production authoring and affected-state
validation. Agreement among specialists does not replace instructor acceptance.
Changes to substantive time, depth, emphasis, delivery or explicit instructor
constraints remain instructor decisions. Later execution can reopen the
smallest affected cluster and owning artifacts; no premature freeze is immunity
from justified backpressure.
