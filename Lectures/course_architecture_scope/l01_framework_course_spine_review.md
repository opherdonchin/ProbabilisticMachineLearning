## 1. The framework students actually encountered in L01

**Primary evidence:** [L01 delivered slides](../L01_Probability_Statistics/slide_deck.typ), source blob \`235b8eeb48ec9075d2bcea82b6a377c0a45b6a79\`, especially *Machine learning defined*, *Task, experience, and performance*, *Experience taxonomy*, *Task taxonomy*, *Learning problems become probabilistic queries*, *Summary: Probability for machine learning*, and *One linear Gaussian system supports different questions*. This framework comes from the actual deck, **not** the earlier L01 coverage specification, which differs in emphasis.

L01 connected familiar Bayesian modeling to a coherent field of machine learning through three linked ideas:

1. **Learning-problem specification:** What is the task, what experience/data/feedback does the learner have, and what performance criterion assesses success? L01 explicitly distinguished prediction, generation, discovery and action, and emphasized that one dataset may support different tasks. The performance measure is not necessarily posterior accuracy or a likelihood.
2. **Probabilistic expression of the task:** Identify observed and unknown variables and formulate a relevant joint/conditional distribution. A task becomes a *probabilistic query*; changing the query can change the computational problem even if the data and underlying model stay fixed.
3. **Reusable mathematical operations:** Conditioning updates a distribution given observations, marginalization integrates away unknowns, and summarization computes a required expectation or decision from a distribution. L01 showed those operations on a tractable linear-Gaussian model and separated preserving posterior uncertainty from extracting a point summary.

L01 **did not yet establish** a general theorem that every ML task reduces to posterior approximation, nor did it teach EM, AD, VI or MCMC correctness. The inferential computational difficulty that unifies later methods is a *course synthesis built on L01*, not a sentence falsely attributed to the original deck.

The recurring question for later lectures is therefore:

> Given the task, available experience and performance criterion, what probabilistic model and query express the problem? Which operations answer the query, which become difficult, and what modeling structure or computational approximation makes progress possible—at what cost?

**Do not reduce this framework to one mandatory lesson template.** Some methods change the representation or expressive class, others enable exact computation, others optimize a point or an approximate distribution, and still others change how success is evaluated. Each can be located in the common framework without treating the methods as interchangeable.

## 3. The developing argument across the semester

A credible semester argument is **not** a straight staircase from "exact posterior" to ever more elaborate "approximate posterior". There are at least three interacting developments:

- **Problem and model specification:** L01 establishes tasks and queries, L02 unifies familiar probability families, L03 reveals graphical factorization, L04 gives information/decision measures, L05 introduces outcome-appropriate GLMs, and L11 adds expressive nonlinear conditional/inference functions. These steps expand *what can be expressed and assessed*.
- **Inference/learning computation:** L01 shows tractable Gaussian operations; L02 makes some fitting analytic through moments; L05 gives point optimization with derivatives; L06 makes latent-variable parameter fitting easier through conditional expectations; L07 restores a local posterior approximation; L08 exploits graph structure for exact marginalization; L09 optimizes an approximate distribution; L10 samples for expected quantities; L12 amortizes latent inference and joint learning. These steps expand *what can be computed*, by different—not equivalent—means.
- **Evaluation and epistemic costs:** L01's task/performance specification reappears in L04 information versus decision risk, L05 held-out log loss, L06 observed likelihood versus reconstruction, L07 accuracy of query-specific integral, L09 restriction/optimization error, L10 Monte Carlo sampling error, L11 predictive distribution and uncertainty attribution, and L12 reconstruction/rate versus generation/identifiability. These are not the same loss and should not be conflated.

**Especially important transitions:**

1. **L05 → L06 → L07:** For one probabilistic modeling goal, move from useful point fitting to latent-variable optimization and then from a point to an approximate posterior shape. Explain explicitly which operation became hard and what a method does/not approximate. Do not imply that general Bayesian modeling "fails".
2. **L03 → L08:** Graph factorization and CI explain why a seemingly exponential marginalization can be reorganized into exact messages. Here a useful *structural* insight avoids the need for approximation.
3. **L06 → L09 → L12:** The same KL/ELBO identity supports very different computational decisions: EM with an exact conditional at fixed point parameters, VI optimizing an approximate posterior, and a VAE with shared, amortized q for each observation.
4. **L05 → L11 → L12:** Differentiable likelihood fitting extends from linear GLMs to expressive neural conditional distributions and then to networks used as both generative decoders and inference encoders.
5. **L04 → L12:** MI/conditional information and communication tradeoffs return as ways to discuss learned representations, but average prior KL is not generally equal to the information carried about inputs.

## 4. Failure modes a source-arc or feasibility review can miss

- **Perfectly feasible, isolated method:** A lecture teaches one algorithm and its notebook expertly but students cannot relate its role to the task/query/operation introduced in L01.
- **False continuity:** "This is Bayesian inference" disguises an actual change of objective—for example EM's point estimate or an MLE optimizer—without explaining what uncertainty was discarded.
- **False separation:** The agent frames every chapter as a completely new *problem*, obscuring that several methods are alternative computational responses to a shared probabilistic modeling goal.
- **False equivalence:** Describing all algorithms as posterior approximations would misrepresent GLM likelihood choices, graphical structure, exact HMM messages, point-only EM, information theory and neural architectures.
- **Review-only slides:** A generic slide reciting L01's categories does not demonstrate conceptual continuity. Require the connection to explain a real mathematical transition or comparison; a single sharp contrast often suffices.
- **Orphan return:** A concept appears in the ledger as "used later" but its original mathematical significance is never explicitly taught anywhere.
